Five jobs, three machines

Add two numbers. Sum 1 to 5 with a loop. Pick the greater of two. Store a value and read it back. Multiply by four. Nothing here needs a library, a framework or a runtime — and that is the point.

Weft-8

The Loom's machine: 16 registers, 27 two-byte opcodes, 256 bytes. This column is executable — the page runs it and the tests assert the result.

x86-64

The machine under most laptops. Hand-written illustrations of the same job, labelled as illustrations rather than passed off as compiler output.

AArch64

The machine in most phones. Same job again — and the closest of the three to the Weft-8 in shape, because both keep their flags in one place.

What you notice after four examples

  • The ideas are all the same. Load, add, compare, branch, store. Every machine on this page is those five things wearing different names.
  • The differences are arithmetic and addressing. x86 subtracts and tests in two instructions; ARM does it in one; the Weft-8 needs three, because SUB sets flags but cannot branch.
  • Size is the visible cost of generality. The Weft-8 reaches memory with a literal byte address; x86 and ARM compute one from a frame or stack pointer, and their instructions grow to say so.
  • Built on the Loom. The Weft-8 column is weft8.js from app-jacquard, byte for byte the same engine.
